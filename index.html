00 About

Trained on the factory floor. Now moving toward the shop floor.

I started in production merchandising — the part of fashion that runs on order confirmations, T&A calendars, and buyer approvals. That's where I learned how a garment actually gets made, and how easily a timeline slips when nobody's watching closely enough.

From there, my interest moved toward the retail side of the business: consumer behaviour, point-of-sale signal, and the decisions that actually determine what gets bought and what gets marked down. Along the way I picked up a habit of building things — small tools and models that take scattered data and turn it into something a person can act on in under a minute.

That's the instinct I want to bring into retail merchandising and buying, where data should meet the decisions that move a brand forward — not sit in a spreadsheet nobody opens.

Shipment Risk & T&A Monitoring Dashboard

Even with a working ERP, CTA's merchandising team had no forward-looking view — just live status, spread across roughly fifteen disconnected files, with shipment risk invisible until a milestone was already missed.

02 Case study — Customer analytics

Segmenting apparel customers to drive merchandising strategy

Retailers collect purchase data on every customer, then act on it in bulk — same discount, same push, regardless of who's actually buying. That's expensive: premium customers get discounted unnecessarily, and price-sensitive ones get ignored until they churn.

Rather than assuming customer "types" upfront, I let purchase frequency and spend reveal natural groupings through K-Means clustering, then layered KNN on top so every new customer gets slotted into the right segment automatically, in real time.

03 Research

A standardised framework for sustainable fashion consumption

NIFT coursework — a critical review and conceptual proposal.

Everyone's measuring sustainability differently — so nothing lines up.

Consumption drives most of a garment's lifetime environmental impact, yet it's the stage the industry measures worst. Tools like the Higg Index score products and factories rigorously, but stop at the point of sale — and can still misrank materials depending on how they're weighted. Academic research fills that gap with self-reported surveys that poorly predict real behaviour, leaving the field contradicting itself on basic questions like whether consumers actually pay more to be sustainable.

My proposal, ISCM, structures the gap into five measurable dimensions: environmental perception, social & ethical perception, cognitive alignment (the gap between what people say and what they do), economic feasibility, and behavioural engagement — the actual outcome.

It's the same instinct behind the CTA dashboard: build a structured, comparable system where none exists, instead of relying on scattered or self-reported inputs.

06 What's next

I've seen how a product gets made. Now I want to own how it sells.

Working inside an export house taught me where product comes from — sourcing, T&A, buyer timelines, the operational cost behind every SKU. What I don't yet have full visibility into is what happens after: how that product performs at the point of sale, and why a consumer picks it up or walks past it.

My graduation project connects POS-level signal — what's actually selling, to whom, how fast — with the buying and assortment decisions merchandisers make upstream. Same instinct as the CTA dashboard and the ISCM research: build the structure when the data exists but isn't usable yet.
